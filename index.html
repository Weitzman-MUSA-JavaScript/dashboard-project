      What do the four groups mean?

      Groups compare firearm assault injury rates in 2020–2022 and 2023–2025 against the same 2015–2019 benchmark.

      Persistently elevated
      At or above the benchmark in both periods.
      Previously elevated
      At or above the benchmark in 2020–2022, below it in 2023–2025.
      Newly elevated
      Below the benchmark in 2020–2022, at or above it in 2023–2025.
      Consistently below
      Below the benchmark in both periods. This does not mean no injuries occurred.

        How are these numbers calculated?

        Summaries are imported from the R workflow.

        ACS: American Community Survey, 2020–2024. SVI: Social Vulnerability Index, 2022, based on ACS 2018–2022. These are snapshots, not demographic trends.

        Translucent bands show approximate 90% ACS sampling uncertainty, not variation between tracts. SVI has no margin of error available here.

        Example: 20% ± 2 percentage points gives an approximate range of 18%–22%.

        Unclassified tracts remain in the map and tract table but are excluded here.

        Methods, sources and downloads

        Elevated burden means meeting the fixed 2015–2019 upper-quartile tract rate benchmark. “Below” does not mean no harm. Special-use and otherwise rate-ineligible tracts are retained as unavailable but their counts remain accessible.

        The weighted view pools ACS counts within each measure’s population or household universe and averages SVI tract ranks using fixed ACS population weights. The typical-tract view gives every tract equal weight.

        • Firearm assault injuries: Philadelphia Police Department’s Shooting Victims dataset via OpenDataPhilly, local extraction May 21, 2026.
        • Census ACS 2020–2024 five-year estimates.
        • CDC/ATSDR SVI 2022 documentation; national tract release.
