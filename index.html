      About the data
      • Grocery access: USDA Economic Research Service, Food Access Research Atlas: SNAP-authorized Retailer Access Map, 2025 (released July 2026), on 2020 census tracts.
      • Store locations: USDA Food and Nutrition Service, Historical SNAP Retailer Locator Data, stores authorized as of December 31, 2025, within 10 miles of the county line, since residents near the edge of the boundary may shop outside city limits. "Grocery stores" are the supermarket, super store and large grocery store types, which matches USDA's Large Retailer Access Map.
      • Population data: U.S. Census Bureau, 2020 Census block populations, via TIGERweb.
      • Income, poverty and vehicles: American Community Survey 2020 to 2024 5-year estimates, as published in the USDA file above.
      • Grocery spending: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Consumer Expenditure Survey 2024, food at home by income quintile, applied to each tract's households by median family income.
      • Opportunity Zones. Office of the Texas Governor, Economic Development and Tourism, Opportunity Zone 2.0 nominated and eligible tracts, September 2026.
      • Neighborhoods: City of Houston Planning and Development, Super Neighborhoods. Outside the city, U.S. Census Bureau 2020 incorporated and census-designated places.
      • Store sales: U.S. Census Bureau, 2022 Economic Census, retail trade sales for Harris County; Texas Comptroller, sales tax receipts by retail subsector, 2022.
      • Tax rates: City of Houston, tax year 2025 adopted property tax rate; Texas Comptroller, sales tax on grocery and convenience stores.
      • Transit: Metropolitan Transit Authority of Harris County, GTFS schedule data, August 30, 2026 service change.
      • A tract is low access when at least 500 people, or a third of its residents, live more than 1 mile from a store (10 miles in rural tracts), measured from 2020 census block centers. Low income comes from the USDA's definition.
      • Plan sales come from a Huff model: each block's grocery spending is split among stores within 5 miles by store size over distance squared. Sales a new store takes from existing stores move around the city instead of adding to it, so only the remainder counts as new city sales tax. Groceries for home use are exempt from Texas sales tax, so only nonfood and prepared items are taxed.
